BANKING SCAM INVESTIGATION GUIDANCE

SECTION 1: OTP AND CREDENTIAL REQUESTS

Banks and legitimate financial institutions generally do not ask customers to disclose OTPs, PINs, CVVs, passwords, or other authentication credentials through unsolicited messages or calls.

Requests for OTPs or authentication credentials should be treated as suspicious indicators during an investigation.

SECTION 2: URGENT ACCOUNT THREATS

Scam messages frequently use urgency or threats such as account suspension, account blocking, immediate verification, or limited-time deadlines.

Urgent language combined with requests for sensitive information can increase the risk associated with an evidence item.

SECTION 3: SUSPICIOUS LINKS

Investigators should examine URLs found in suspicious messages.

Warning indicators can include unfamiliar domains, misleading domain names, URL shorteners, requests to verify accounts, or links that direct users to login or verification pages.

The presence of a URL alone does not prove that a message is fraudulent. URL indicators should be considered together with other evidence.

SECTION 4: PAYMENT REQUESTS

Requests to transfer money, send funds to unfamiliar accounts, or make payments under unusual circumstances should be investigated carefully.

Payment-related evidence should be correlated with transaction details, recipient information, timestamps, and other available evidence.

SECTION 5: SOCIAL ENGINEERING

Social engineering attacks may impersonate banks, government agencies, delivery services, employers, or other trusted organizations.

Common techniques include urgency, authority claims, fear of account loss, requests for confidential information, and instructions to follow unfamiliar links.

SECTION 6: INVESTIGATOR REVIEW

Automated risk analysis should support investigators rather than replace human review.

Risk indicators should be traceable to evidence observed in the case.

Investigators should review the original evidence, extracted entities, deterministic risk indicators, and relevant supporting guidance before making a final case decision.
